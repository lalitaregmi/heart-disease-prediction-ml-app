Understanding Heart Disease

Learn about the causes, types, and impact of heart disease to better protect yourself and your loved ones.

What is Heart Disease?

Heart disease refers to several types of heart conditions. The most common type is coronary artery disease, which affects blood flow to the heart and can lead to heart attacks.

Heart disease develops when the arteries that supply blood to the heart muscle become hardened and narrowed due to a buildup of plaque on the arteries' inner walls. This buildup reduces blood flow to the heart, which can cause chest pain (angina), shortness of breath, or a heart attack.

Other types of heart disease include heart failure, arrhythmia (abnormal heart rhythms), and heart valve problems.

Heart Disease Statistics

  • Heart disease is the leading cause of death globally
  • Nearly 18 million people die from cardiovascular diseases annually
  • In the United States, someone has a heart attack every 40 seconds
  • About 1 in 5 heart attacks is silent—the person isn't aware of damage to their heart

Types of Heart Disease

Coronary Artery Disease

The most common type, caused by plaque buildup in the arteries that supply blood to the heart. This can lead to heart attacks if blood flow is severely restricted or blocked.

Arrhythmia

Abnormal heart rhythms that can cause the heart to beat too fast, too slow, or irregularly. Some arrhythmias can be life-threatening if they prevent the heart from pumping enough blood.

Heart Failure

Occurs when the heart can't pump enough blood to meet the body's needs. It doesn't mean the heart has stopped working, but that it's not working as efficiently as it should.

Valvular Heart Disease

Affects one or more of the four valves that keep blood flowing in the right direction through the heart. Valves may not open or close properly, disrupting blood flow.

Congenital Heart Defects

Heart abnormalities present at birth. They can affect the heart walls, valves, and blood vessels. Some defects are simple with no symptoms, while others are complex and require treatment.

Cardiomyopathy

Disease of the heart muscle that makes it harder for the heart to pump blood to the rest of the body. Can lead to heart failure if not treated.

Common Symptoms of Heart Disease

Chest Pain or Discomfort

Pressure, tightness, pain, or a squeezing or aching sensation in your chest or arms that may spread to your neck, jaw or back.

Shortness of Breath

Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath, which may occur with or without chest discomfort.

Fatigue

Unexplained fatigue or weakness, which may become more severe during physical activity.

Irregular Heartbeat

Heart palpitations or feeling like your heart is racing, fluttering, or beating irregularly.

Warning

Heart attack symptoms vary between individuals. If you suspect you or someone else is having a heart attack, call emergency services immediately. Every minute matters.

Diagnosis and Treatment

Diagnosis Methods

  • Physical Examination: Checking blood pressure, weight, and listening to heart and lungs.
  • Electrocardiogram (ECG): Records electrical signals as they travel through your heart.
  • Stress Test: Monitors your heart during exercise to see how it responds to physical stress.
  • Echocardiogram: Uses sound waves to create images of your heart in motion.
  • Blood Tests: Check for specific proteins that indicate heart damage.

Treatment Options

  • Medications: Various drugs can treat different aspects of heart disease, including blood thinners, beta blockers, and cholesterol-lowering medications.
  • Lifestyle Changes: Healthy diet, regular exercise, quitting smoking, and limiting alcohol can significantly improve heart health.
  • Medical Procedures: Angioplasty, stent placement, or bypass surgery may be needed to improve blood flow.
  • Devices: Pacemakers or implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs) may be used to help maintain normal heart rhythm.
  • Heart Transplant: In severe cases where other treatments haven't worked, a heart transplant may be considered.
